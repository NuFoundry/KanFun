# The interview

One message per stage, numbered questions, a default in brackets where one is sensible. The user's
first message usually answers several; skip those and say what you took from it. Never ask what the
research can find out.

## §0 The brief (before any research)

1. **The idea in one sentence.** "An app like X for Y" is enough; what does the user do with it, and
   what is the outcome they pay for?
2. **The market.** Which countries or languages first, and why those [the user's own]? Businesses
   or consumers; which size or kind?
3. **You.** Who is building this: one person, a team, with what time per week and what money for
   the first year? What are you good at, and what do you not want to do (sales calls, support,
   content)? Do you have an unfair advantage: domain knowledge, an audience, customers waiting,
   being the customer yourself?
4. **What exists.** Code, a prototype, a domain, customers, a brand, documents (business case,
   requirements, research): name them, or drop them into `strategy/` or `references/`.
5. **Constraints.** Must-haves (a language, an integration, a law), no-gos (a region, a customer
   type, a business model), a date that matters (a launch, a regulation, running out of runway).
6. **Success.** What does good look like in 12 and in 36 months: a salary, a stable side business,
   a company that gets funded or sold? How much effort should it take to run once it works?
7. **What you already believe.** The niche, the competitor to beat, the price, the channel: say it
   as beliefs. The research tests them; a belief that survives becomes a direction, one that does
   not saves months.

Write `strategy/brief.md`: the answers in the user's words under those seven headings, with the
beliefs listed as **hypotheses**. Read it back in one paragraph and ask if it is right.

## §1 Before the research

Only if the brief leaves one of these open, and only in one message: the geography when "Europe"
or "the US" needs a first country; whether a named competitor is the one to study or just an
example; a term that means different things in different industries.

## §3 The direction

Find the directions on the table from 03 before asking anything; the user picks from a table,
not from an open question. Always include:

- the idea as the user stated it;
- a narrower version: one segment, one country, one job, which the research suggests is winnable;
- the adjacent business the research surfaced: the same product sold to a different buyer, the
  channel partner's side, the hand-off into a tool the segment already uses;
- the one that looks big but is a trap (a funded incumbent, a market that needs a sales team the
  user does not want), named so the user does not drift into it later.

The table compares them on: who pays, what it takes to build, competition, time to first revenue,
the effort to run it, who has to be convinced, fit with what exists, upside **(est.)**, verdict.
Recommend one and say why in three sentences. Then ask only:

1. Which direction? [the recommendation]
2. The ideal first customer: does the one in the table match someone you know by name?
3. Are the kill criteria ones you would act on?

## §4 The plan's numbers

Ask for what only the user knows; propose the rest from 03's pricing benchmark and say so.

1. Prices and plans [from 03 §8, positioned where the direction says]; monthly or yearly; a trial
   without a card, with a card, or a free tier?
2. Paid acquisition budget for the first 12 months, and the month it may start [€0 until 02 §9 is
   met].
3. The founder's salary the business should cover, and from when.
4. Running costs the user already pays (tools, accounting, insurance) [the template's stack costs
   €20–100 a month at the start].
5. Help: freelancers, a co-founder, an agency, or none?

Everything else in the model is an assumption with **(est.)**: conversion, churn and growth come
from 03's benchmarks and the comparable businesses in the report, in three scenarios.

## §6 Scope

Ask only when the roadmap needs a decision the documents do not settle:

1. A must-have from the brief that 00's scope guard puts out of scope: keep it, hand it off to a
   tool that does it, or drop it?
2. The launch date 04 should plan backwards from, if the brief gave none.
3. What the user can build themselves versus what needs a vendor or a partner (payment, tax,
   e-invoicing, a data source).

## The checkpoint message

After each document, in this order and nothing more:

- What it concluded, in at most three sentences.
- The decisions it needs, numbered, each with the recommendation.
- Where to look if they want detail (the file and the section).
- "Read it, then say go for `<next document>`."
